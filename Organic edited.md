# Names and formulae of Organic Compounds

## What is organic Chemistry?

- Chemistry of carbon containing compounds
- What are the 3 special features of carbon?
    - can join together forming long chain
    - carbon atoms can be linked by single or double covalent bonds
    - can arrange themselves in rings
- Carbon forms four covalent bonds
- The ability to form many different bonds of carbon is called catenation

### What are the different types of formulae?

- **Empirical Formula**: Smallest whole number ratio of the atoms of each element in a compound
- **Molecular Formula**: Gives the number of atoms of each element in one molecule of a substance
- **Structural Formula**: Shows how groups of atoms are arranged in a molecule
- **Displayed Formula (Full Structural Formula)**: Shows all the atoms and all the bonds in one molecule of a compound.

### What are hydrocarbons?

- Compounds that contain only hydrogen and carbon atoms
- Two types:
    - **Saturated Hydrocarbon**: Contains carbon-carbon single bond only
    - **Unsaturated Hydrocarbon**: All carbon bonds may not be single bonds
- examples include:
    - alkanes
    - alkenes
    - alkynes

# Homologous Series of an organic compound

- a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties due to the presence of same functional group
- They have the following properties:
    - same functional groups
    - same general formula
    - displays trend in physical properties
    - shares similar chemical properties
    - differs from one member to another successive member by a -CH₂- unit
- as carbon number increases in a homologous series:
    - viscosity increases
    - MP/BP increases
    - intermolecular forces increases
*Viscosity: measure of a fluid's resistance to flow*
### What is Unsaturation Test? (alkene test)

# Structural Isomerism

### What are Structural Isomers?

- compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae and therefore different displayed formulae

### What are the three types of structural isomers?

- **Position isomers**: Change the position of the functional group
- **Branching**: Branch a CH₃ group or another
- **Changing**: alkane to cyclo-alkane

### How do branched hydrocarbons differ in physical properties?

- Hydrocarbons containing branched chains have lower MP/BP than straight chain hydrocarbons

# Alkanes

- Saturated hydrocarbon
- doesn't have any functional group
- **General Formula**: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
- Contains carbon-carbon single bond only
- Ends with -ane
- relatively unreactive due to only single bonds between carbons
- Low Melting and Boiling Points
- ![image.png](image_1742653131963_0.png)
- ![image.png](image_1742653205206_0.png)
- LPG has propane and butane

## What are the Reactions of Alkanes?

### Combustion

- react with oxygen or air and thus used as fuels
- Alkanes burn very exothermally
- **complete combustion** occurs in excess oxygen. products are carbon dioxide and water.
    - ![image.png](image_1742655926557_0.png)
- **incomplete combustion** occurs in limited oxygen or air supply, products are carbon monoxide (toxic) and water.
    - ![image.png](image_1742655908614_0.png)
    - incomplete combustion produces soot (carbon particles). Glows yellow in flame gives safety burner the yellow flame

### Substitution with chlorine

- **substitution reaction**: one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another atom or group of atoms
- a photochemical reaction
    - a chemical reaction initiated by light
    - in this is Ultra Violet (UV)
- one chlorine atom replaces a hydrogen atom from the alkane
- the organic product from alkane is chloromethane CH₃Cl
- ![image.png](image_1742656365750_0.png)
- unless chlorine is limited the substitution reaction goes on until tetrachloromethane is formed.
- ![image.png](image_1742656385745_0.png#invert_dark)

# Alcohols

- Contains OH⁻ functional group (hydroxyl group)
- **General Formula**: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH
- Their empirical formulae differs for every member in the alcohol homologous series unlike alkanes, alkenes and alkynes.
    - Methanol: CH₄O
    - Ethanol: C₂H₆O
    - Propanol: C₃H₈O
- ends with -anol (-diol if two alcohol functional groups present)
- Early alcohol series are neutral
- Colorless liquids that do not conduct electricity
- Simplest molecule is methanol

## Manufacture of Ethanol

- large manufacture of ethanol is done by fermentation of carbohydrates and catalytic addition of steam to ethene

### Fermentation of carbohydrates

- carbohydrates like sugar are down by enzymes in yeast to produce glucose C₆H₁₂O₆
- the enzymes also catalyse the breakdown of glucose to form ethanol and carbon dioxide
- **Conditions**:
    - 25-35°C
    - anaerobic conditions (if O₂ present then ethanol turns to ethanoic acid)
    - Yeast
    - room pressure
- ![image.png](image_1742668000548_0.png)
- when ethanol concentration reaches 14% it kills the yeast (denatures the active site of enzyme)
- Yeast cells are removed by filtration and purifies by fractional distillation
- carried out in the following laboratory apparatus:
- ![image.png](image_1742706420845_0.png)
- airlock used so that CO₂ escapes and no gases come in and prevail anaerobic conditions

### Catalytic addition of steam to ethene (hydration)

- catalyst: phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄)
- temperature of 300°C
- pressure of 60 atm or 6000kPa
- ![image.png](image_1742668119814_0.png)

### So which process is better?

|**Hydration of ethene**|**Fermentation**|
|---|---|
|uses non-renewable resources|uses renewable resources|
|continuous process|batch process|
|fast rate of reaction|relatively slow rate of reaction|
|high purity ethanol yield|ethanol produced must be filtered and purified by fractional distillation|
|sophisticated method|simple process|

# Petrochemicals

- Three major fossil fuels are:

    - petroleum
- they are finite and non-renewable
- all contains hydrocarbons
- Methane is the main constituent of natural gas

### Petroleum

- **Petroleum (Crude Oil)**: comes from remains of dead marine microorganisms
- petroleum is a mixture of many hydrocarbons

#### How is petroleum formed?

- prehistoric marine creatures die → sink in seabed → gets covered by mud and earth → high pressure, temperature and bacterial activity over millions of years → organic material broken down to hydrocarbons
- geological movements and pressure create reservoirs of oil and gas
- Petroleum is refined through fractional distillation
- ![image.png](image_1742707715876_0.png)
- top to bottom:
    - viscosity decreases
    - chain length decreases
    - BP decreases
    - volatility increases

### Cracking of petroleum

- long chain hydrocarbons are in less demand than short chain hydrocarbon
- so they are broken down to short chain hydrocarbons
- decomposition reaction as carbon-carbon bonds break to form smaller molecules
- ![image.png](image_1742664269816_0.png)
- **Conditions**:
    - 500°C
    - Silica, Alumina or Zeolite as catalyst
- ![image.png](image_1742705818671_0.png)
- fractional distillation carried out on the mixture to obtain certain fraction
- the hydrogen obtained can be used as fuel or in haber process

# Polymers

- **Polymers**: large molecules made when small molecules called monomers take part in polymerisation reaction
- **Monomers**: small molecules which are used to form polymers
- **Natural Polymers**: Proteins, Carbohydrates, Wood and natural rubber
- **Synthetic Polymers**: Plastics
- **Homo-Polymers**: contains one polymer only
- **Co-Polymers**: contains more than one monomer
- **Polymerisation**: reaction through which monomers join to form polymers

## What are the two types of polymerisation?

### Addition Polymerisation

- Alkenes double bonds break under high temperature and pressure in presence of catalyst
- many alkene molecules go through the same and they all link up together to form poly(alkene)
- addition polymerisation as many molecules add up together
- ![image.png](image_1742666735401_0.png)
- ![image.png](image_1742666792160_0.png)

#### Features of addition polymerisation

- all are long chain molecules made by joining together large number of monomer molecules
- includes monomers with C=C molecules
- are homopolymers
- double bonds breakup to join to other monomers

#### Environmental challenges posed by plastics

##### Disposal

- often disposed by burying them in landfills, where plastics may remain in the environment and take up large amounts of space
- incineration (burning) lead to the production of toxic gases like CO and acidic gases like hydrogen chloride, which leads to acid rain

##### How to overcome these problems?

- development of biodegradable plastics (broken down by bacterial activity)
- photodegradable plastics (broken down by light)
- sorting and recycling schemes

##### Accumulation in oceans

- cause harm to marine life

##### How to overcome?

- decrease manufacture of single use plastic
- using materials other than plastics
